## Analyst brief (27 Sep to 3 Oct vs 20 to 26 Sep)

Visitors counted only where country is GB or IE (a scraper bot sends fake visits from elsewhere).

**Social reach:** Facebook post impressions 3,766 (was 9,684), followers 666 (+14). Instagram reach 971 (was 6,292), followers 918 (+21). Both prior weeks were inflated by one-day spikes (21 Sep Facebook, 20 Sep Instagram), so the drop is mostly the outlier going. TikTok followers 974 (+63), 8 videos, 2,420 views at video level. YouTube Shorts/video views 1,296 (was 1). Threads tiny (42 views). Pinterest and LinkedIn: no data yet (not live last week).

**Site:** about 360 visitors and 49 sign-ups (was 419 and 66). 85 to 90 percent of traffic has no UTM tag (direct, Google, other). Google referrals are rising (13 to 26 a day) while sign-ups fell, so name searches are growing but not converting as fast.

**Worked:** the "is the map near you full of pins or a bit empty" post was the top Facebook post (18 shares, 39 clicks, 2,097 impressions). Instagram link in bio was the best converter (prior week 10 visits, 4 sign-ups). TikTok "What's your Record?" (450 views, 21 interactions) and the YouTube "Where Coeliacs find places to eat" video (1,007 views) led the video formats. Sign-ups peaked 24, 26 and 27 Sep (13, 19, 13 a day).

**Flopped:** supermarket, takeaway and myth posts brought 1 visitor each. No tracked traffic from Pinterest yet. Only 1 of 47 new members went on to a vouch or venue page.

**Likely reason:** people who click are curious about their own area; people who sign up come via Google or the Instagram bio after seeing a video. The biggest drop-off is homepage to register page (about 23 percent of homepage visitors), but nearly everyone on the register page finishes.

**Do more of:** map and "near you" posts, screen-recording videos of the map, a vouch nudge for new members (almost none vouch yet), and Instagram link-in-bio calls to action.

### 2026-10-22 thu-venues
What coeliac-friendly venues get right, and it’s not the menu.

They write it down. The answer to “is that coeliac-friendly?” is the same whoever is on shift, because it isn’t in one person’s head. It’s on a card by the till or in a folder in the kitchen.

They separate. Fryer, toaster, boards, utensils. Not as a favour, as routine.

They make it easy to ask. A line on the menu saying “tell us if you’re coeliac” does more than any logo.

They don’t over-promise. “We take care, and here’s how” beats a big claim every time. Coeliacs notice.

None of this costs much. All of it earns the kind of repeat custom that’s hard to buy.

If your team does these things, let coeliacs know.
